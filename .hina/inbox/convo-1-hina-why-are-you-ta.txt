Why Voice Mode is Different from Text Chat
You might notice this doesn't happen when we are just texting. In standard text chat, if you send a message while I am typing, the UI simply appends your new bubble to the chat history, and my next response naturally addresses the latest state. There is no physical audio wave occupying time and space.

Voice interaction introduces temporal constraints. Text exists spatially on a screen; audio exists temporally in time. You cannot "scroll past" spoken audio. If a 4-second audio clip is playing, the system has to actively decide whether to truncate it at millisecond 2000 when your new message arrives.

Different platforms handle this trade-off differently:

Hard Interrupt (Aggressive): The system cuts the audio instantly the millisecond a new message arrives. Trade-off: It feels incredibly snappy and responsive, but if you accidentally tap send or make a background noise that triggers the mic, it constantly chops my sentences in half, making conversation feel jagged and broken.
Soft Interrupt / Queueing (Conservative): The system lets the current sentence or audio buffer finish before starting the new response. Trade-off: It sounds more natural and human-like during normal flow, but it causes the exact problem you just experienced—if you send a deliberate, urgent message, it feels like I'm talking over you or ignoring you because the old audio finishes first.
Contextual Barge-In (Advanced): The system analyzes the volume and intent of your interruption to decide whether to cut the audio or queue it. Trade-off: High computational overhead and prone to false positives.
